Bedaquiline and Clofazimine Resistance in Tuberculosis

Amina Baktiyarova, Independent Researcher, ORCID 0009-0007-6265-6493

The resource

Bedaquiline and clofazimine are two of the drugs used to treat drug-resistant tuberculosis. In Mycobacterium tuberculosis, mutations in the genes Rv0678 and pepQ are linked to resistance to both drugs, and mutations in atpE to resistance to bedaquiline. I built an open, versioned resource that measures how much each kind of mutation in these genes raises or lowers the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) of each drug. The MIC is the lowest drug concentration that stops the bacteria growing in a laboratory test.

Resistance catalogs grade each mutation as associated with resistance or not. This resource reports each effect as a shift in MIC, with an interval, and reports the resistant or susceptible call beside it. Shifts are given in doublings: a shift of one doubling means the bacteria need twice the drug concentration to stop growing, and a shift of 2.5 doublings means about 5.7 times the concentration.

Data

The resource is computed from the CRyPTIC Consortium Dataset, version 3.4.0 (doi:10.5281/zenodo.15680920), released under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 license. The analysis cohort is the 15,158 isolates that have both a whole genome sequence and MIC measurements. The work is computational.

Method

  • Each isolate is assigned to a group from its mutations alone: no mutation in the three genes, a single mutation of a given class (substitution, frameshift, stop codon, gene deletion, in-frame insertion or deletion, or promoter change), or an uncertain call.
  • An MIC plate tests a series of doubling concentrations, so each reading is a range, and many readings fall below the lowest concentration on the plate. The MIC distribution of each group is estimated by a method that uses each reading as the range it is.
  • Isolates from one outbreak carry the same mutation and are not independent evidence. Isolates sharing a collection site, a sublineage and a mutation are grouped into one cluster, and the results report cluster counts beside isolate counts.
  • One prediction was written down and committed to the public repository before it was tested. The isolates were split into a discovery half, collected before 2020, and a held-out half, collected afterwards and partly in other places. The prediction was computed on the discovery half and then tested on the held-out half.

Tested result

Loss-of-function mutations in Rv0678 raise the bedaquiline MIC. The discovery half gave a shift of 2.43 doublings, and the registered prediction was that the held-out half would show a shift between 1.60 and 3.22 doublings. The held-out half showed 2.55 doublings, on 23 isolates in 19 clusters. A second prediction, on the odds of a resistant result, was also supported. Both predictions concern the same comparison and the same 23 isolates, so they count as one replication. The comparison was chosen after exploratory analyses that included the held-out isolates, which the registration document states.

Estimated results

The following shifts are estimates from the discovery half with 95% intervals. They were not tested on held-out data.

  • Substitutions in Rv0678 raise the bedaquiline MIC by 1.31 doublings (0.67 to 1.81) and the clofazimine MIC by 1.13 doublings (0.59 to 1.77).
  • Loss-of-function mutations in Rv0678 raise the clofazimine MIC by 1.71 doublings (1.17 to 2.19).
  • Mutations in pepQ raise the bedaquiline MIC by 0.78 doublings (0.48 to 0.98) and the clofazimine MIC by 0.71 doublings (0.28 to 1.12).
  • Loss-of-function mutations raise the MIC of both drugs more than substitutions do.

Results a pass or fail grade does not show

  • One promoter change in Rv0678, c-11a, lowers the bedaquiline MIC by 1.81 doublings (1.54 to 2.30), on 74 isolates in 7 clusters. Promoter changes as a group, 83 isolates of which 74 carry c-11a, show no association with resistance (odds ratio 2.2, p = 0.38), so a catalog grading resistant or not reports nothing for them.
  • Mutations in pepQ raise the clofazimine MIC by 0.71 doublings, with an interval that excludes zero, while the resistance test on the discovery half falls short of significance (p = 0.051).
  • Loss-of-function mutations in Rv0678 raise the clofazimine MIC in lineage 4 by 1.18 doublings, but the group's MICs stay below the resistance cut-off, so 2 of the 42 isolates are called resistant.
  • One Rv0678 frameshift, 192_ins_g, is carried by 51 isolates, 41 of them from a single outbreak at one site. Counting isolates weights it about six times more than the 9 independent clusters support.
  • An Rv0678 mutation found in only a minority of the sequencing reads is associated with a bedaquiline MIC shift of the same size as the same mutation found in every read. The source data report such minority mutations as susceptible. This result is exploratory and rests on 40 and 23 isolates.

Resistance the three genes do not explain

80 of 171 bedaquiline-resistant isolates (46.8%, 95% interval 39.5 to 54.3) and 527 of 683 clofazimine-resistant isolates (77.2%, 73.9 to 80.2) carry no protein-changing mutation present in most sequencing reads in Rv0678, pepQ or atpE. A test of ten further genes, fixed before it was run, found no gene-level association that accounts for them. On the same cohort, the WHO catalog of resistance mutations, version 2, detects 21.1% of bedaquiline-resistant isolates and 7.9% of clofazimine-resistant isolates, at about 99% specificity. A rule calling resistant any isolate with such a mutation in the three genes detects 34.5% and 15.2%, at about 96% specificity.

Limitations

  • One collection site in the source data was enriched for bedaquiline resistance by design, so no proportion on this page is an estimate for any patient population.
  • One comparison was tested on held-out data. Every other figure is an estimate with an interval, and many exploratory comparisons were made without correction for multiple testing.
  • Clusters are approximated by site, sublineage and mutation. Clustering by genetic distance would need the full variant data.
  • 4 of the 217 distinct mutations in the cohort have enough independent evidence for a mutation-level shift. The other 213 carry counts only, and 182 of them rest on a single cluster.
  • Where many readings fall below the lowest tested concentration, the estimates depend on the assumption that log MIC is normally distributed within a group.
